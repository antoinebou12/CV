I had boards leaning in every corner, so I designed a **vertical rack** in CAD, cut **angled slots** in plywood, and built something that could hold **skateboards and a snowboard** without drilling into the rental walls. This is the December 2021 project, story posts cropped to the actual work. **[Version française]({{< ref "/posts/skate-rack-cad-to-object/index.fr.md" >}})**.

<!--more-->

## Design in CAD

Two side panels, three horizontal ties, and **six downward-angled slots** per side so decks slide in and stay put. Modeling first let me sanity-check spacing for wheels and guards before I bought sheet goods.

![CAD model of vertical six-slot skateboard rack](./images/skate-rack-cad-model.jpeg)

The CAD render is the optimistic version — clean edges, no sawdust, no cat.

## Metal frame detour (shop class energy)

Before the plywood version landed, I welded a **tubular orange frame** in a school shop — good practice for squareness, overkill for an apartment rack. It never became the final bedroom solution, but it proved the upright + cross-member idea.

![Orange tubular metal rack frame in a workshop](./images/skate-rack-metal-frame-shop.jpeg)

## Cutting the slots

Each slot is a **diagonal notch** with a rounded bottom: drill the end, circular saw the straights, file the burrs. Clamps, scrap pile, repeat. This photo is the honest middle — not pretty, just progress.

![Plywood side panel with diagonal slots cut on a workbench](./images/skate-rack-slot-cutting-workshop.jpeg)

## Assembly and inspection

Dry-fit the sides and shelves, stain on a tarp in the garage (December = cold fingers), and let the **quality inspector** (black-and-white cat) sign off by sitting on whatever you just aligned.

![Plywood skate rack frame during assembly](./images/skate-rack-plywood-assembly.jpeg)

![Cat sitting on a shelf of the unfinished skate rack](./images/skate-rack-build-quality-inspector.jpeg)

## Finished rack

Six slots per column, **snowboard on top** (Union bindings visible), skateboards below, one more on the floor. Light plywood, wall-friendly footprint, and everything off the floor except the spare deck.

![Finished wooden skate and snowboard rack with boards loaded](./images/skate-rack-finished-loaded.jpeg)

## What I would change next time

- **Slot angle:** slightly steeper so longboards do not stick out as far.
- **Hardwood edge banding** on the slot mouths — plywood splinters grip tape over time.
- **Wall cleat** instead of leaning — I was renting; today I would anchor properly.
