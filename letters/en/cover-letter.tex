I am a software engineer working toward a master's in information technology engineering at ÉTS. Most of my recent hands-on time is platforms and backends (Kubernetes, Terraform, GitLab CI/CD, GCP, Java, Kotlin, Python) with DevSecOps from research, industry, and teaching. I am looking for platform, build/CI, or liveops roles in game studios---pipelines, artifacts, environments, and the kind of on-call diagnosability you need when a build graph is long and a live service is noisy. I also keep a smaller research thread in computer graphics (OpenGL, C++, Blender). The PDF résumé and \href{https://antoineboucher.info}{antoineboucher.info} spell out the rest.

At IMC2---the Institut multidisciplinaire en cybersécurité et cyberrésilience---I was a part-time research assistant through early 2026. I kept GitLab CI/CD and runners working for services on GCP, used Terraform for rollouts, ran containerized workloads, and tightened Java microservices with automated tests while lining Android builds up with the same promotion path. I contributed to analyst-facing tooling that scores and triages cybersecurity risks at scale. It is not a shipped game credit, but it is the same family of problems: many moving binaries, promotion paths that must stay boring, and services you debug from logs when users are waiting.

I have also been a teaching assistant for LOG8100 DevSecOps at Polytechnique Montréal and for several software-heavy courses at ÉTS: shared GitLab spaces for many concurrent teams, CI templates, runners, Azure-backed labs, and steady coaching on Docker, Kubernetes, databases, backends, and secure delivery when deadlines are fixed. I default to naming the next concrete step after I explain the idea.

Earlier roles include cloud work at IONODES, Kotlin and Spring services at Intact, and contract full-stack work with Django, Vue, observability tooling, and APIs. I picked up production habits I still lean on: narrow logs, dashboards that answer real questions, migrations you can back out of, and scope conversations that do not end in a surprise outage.

Outside that cadence I work on interactive graphics and surface-wear simulation. In public I maintain \href{https://github.com/antoinebou12/uml-mcp}{\texttt{uml-mcp}}, MCP-oriented diagram tooling, because assistants behave more predictably when the host exposes clear tool contracts.

I want to land in a games team that funds platform and release work, writes decisions in issues or merge requests, and treats code review as a teaching loop more than a gate. Thank you for reading; I would welcome a conversation about how this background fits your build farm, deployment story, or live stack.
